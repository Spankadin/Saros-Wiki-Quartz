[[The Cast Lot]], commonly called **the Lot**, regulates and profits from organized gaming across much of civilized [[A Brief Saros History|Saros]]. Its maxim is **“Once cast, the lot stands.”**

Its influence is strongest in [[Rore]]. The Lot charters exactly one official gambling hall in any city or town it serves. House games, tavern games, and street wagers may exist, but they are unsanctioned and receive no protection from the Lot.

Every chartered gambling hall is also a branch of the Lot's banking network, allowing depositors to move wealth between distant settlements without carrying all of it on the road. There is no separate Bank of Saros organization: the hall, its counting rooms, and the Lot's reputation are the bank.

## What the Lot Does

The Lot is concerned with:

- Recognized gaming rules
- House licensing
- Tournament organization
- Large wager verification
- Debt records
- Professional dealers
- Arbitration over disputed games
- Protection against obvious cheating
- Deposits, secured storage, currency exchange, and verified inter-hall drafts

The Lot's involvement does not make gambling safe or fair in every establishment. It means there is someone powerful enough to care when cheating damages the reputation of profitable gaming.

## Appearance and Habits

Professionals of the Lot tend toward richer, cleaner, and more elaborate dress than ordinary merchants. Tailored coats, immaculate cuffs, fine waistcoats, polished jewelry, house tokens, cards, dice, and discreet banking seals all serve as signs that games and money are being handled under recognized authority.

The effect is controlled rather than gaudy. A successful dealer or banker wants every visible detail to suggest confidence, cleanliness, memory, and command of the room.

## Banking Through the Halls

Lot halls commonly provide:

- Coin deposits and withdrawals
- Secured storage for compact valuables
- Currency exchange and appraisal
- Verified drafts honored at another chartered hall
- Escrow for wagers and contracts
- Loans backed by property, cargo, or reputation

The Lot does not promise equal service to everyone. Large withdrawals may require notice, identities may be verified by private methods, and disputed accounts can be frozen until the Lot's arbiters decide who is lying.

Every branch is also a gambling hall. The Lot argues that this concentrates security, trustworthy counting staff, and liquid coin in one place. Critics observe that it also places lenders beside people making poor decisions.

## Rore Headquarters and the House Table

The Lot's headquarters is [[The Crowned Die]] in [[Rore]]. Its upper chambers hold the **House Table**, the Lot's senior governing council and the body through which it governs much of the independent city. The House Table is not a separate faction.

Great gaming houses, lenders, brokers, ward representatives, and commercial interests bargain at the Table. They agree above all that Rore should not be governed by the Crown. Decisions are reached through traded support rather than decrees, and enforcement works when enough wards and houses benefit from the same bargain.

[[Cassia Rook]] serves as **First Dealer**, presiding over the House Table, representing the Lot in civic affairs, and acting as the closest office Rore accepts to a mayor. A First Dealer who repeatedly damages business can lose support quickly.

[[Varo Quill]] runs the Crowned Die itself: its tables, tournaments, counting rooms, staff, guests, and daily banking business. Cassia governs the institution and city coalition; Varo operates the hall that houses them.

The public courts, petition desks, and civic records remain at [[The Dealhouse]], but the Lot's factional headquarters and senior council are at the Crowned Die.

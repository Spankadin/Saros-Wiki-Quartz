This note is the canonical working source for travel, trade routes, settlement spacing, and population in [[A Brief Saros History|Saros]]. Route estimates should be revised here first and then reflected in the relevant settlement notes.

The familiar continental chart, [[Map of Saros|Perla's Map of Saros]], was commissioned by [[Perla Mudridge]] and drawn by [[Steev'rix the Explorer]]. It is a traveler's map, not a modern survey. Roads, coasts, rivers, and mountain symbols communicate what a traveler encounters without reproducing every bend or change of grade.

> **Working map scale:** one small hex is approximately **5 miles** across; one large hex, composed of smaller hexes, is approximately **30 miles** across.

# Size of Saros

| Measure | Working Estimate |

A true end-to-end crossing is a major undertaking of roughly six to ten weeks on connected roads and considerably longer through wilderness, but ordinary travel between neighboring centers does not require months.

## Cartographic Error Allowance

Allow roughly **±15–20%** when measuring directly from the map. Winding roads, switchbacks, river bends, symbolic settlement placement, distorted coastlines, abandoned routes, and Steev'rix's ordinary artistic error all affect the result. The ranges below follow visible routes rather than straight lines wherever those routes have been audited.

# Roads, Safety, and the Legion

Cities and large towns are the strongest islands of [[The Aegis Legion|Legion]] control. Primary roads extend that control as a chain rather than as an unbroken blanket.

- Important roads usually have an inn, hamlet, toll post, fortified farm, or small garrison every **20–25 miles**.
- Secondary roads may run **30–40 miles** between dependable shelters.
- Patrols suppress open raiding but do not prevent bandit roadblocks, orc attacks, extortion, washed-out bridges, or local disputes.
- Farms and villages near a road can usually send for help. Outlying settlements mostly protect themselves or quietly rely on [[The Black Circle]].
- Historical hostility, poor maintenance, difficult terrain, and local governance can make one road much slower or less safe than another of similar length.

Road mileage therefore measures the route, while travel time reflects armor, encumbrance, mounts, weather, terrain, and the willingness to press onward after dusk.

# Rivers and Sailing Without Engines

The rivers drawn on Perla's map are only Saros's predominant waterways. Many are at least one small hex—roughly five miles—wide and behave more like long inland seas than ordinary rivers. They are deep enough for substantial vessels, but depth does not make their currents harmless.

No steam engine is required for upstream traffic. Sarosian crews combine:

- Sail and careful tacking when the river wind cooperates
- **Cordelling**, in which crews or hired teams haul a vessel from a cleared bank with a long rope
- **Warping**, using ropes, capstans, anchor rings, and prepared hauling posts
- **Kedging**, carrying an anchor ahead in a ship's boat and hauling the vessel toward it
- Ship's boats towing during difficult approaches
- Poles and sweeps for small craft and close maneuvering

[[Port Quartz]] maintains cleared hauling reaches, capstans, anchor rings, pilots, and labor contracts along the worst approaches. Large ships can ascend, but they may wait for wind, unload part of their cargo, or spend much of their crew's strength on the river. Downstream travel is faster but more dangerous near bends, shoals, and the confluence.

The river descending from the northern [[The Masys Mountains|Masys Mountains]] is the [[Dun'Nala]], Sarosian Dwarvish for “the Dwarves' Water.” The [[Ascarid]], Elvish for “Rushing-waters,” runs from the [[Lake of Lament]] and its [[Great Fall]]. From their confluence at Port Quartz southward, the joined river is commonly called [[The Coldwater]].

# Major Northern Travel Corridors

## The Western Pocket

[[Kylor]], [[Skaggerty]], [[Goslow]], [[Pyrris]], and the country northwest of Skaggerty occupy a western pocket bounded by broad water. There is no direct Kylor–Goslow road: all ordinary road traffic passes through Skaggerty.

### Kylor to Goslow by Water

**Safe sailing distance:** approximately **110–125 miles**. Kylori boats must negotiate the rough northern edge of [[The Sparkling Sea]], enter [[Bishop's Bay]] through the western approach, and pass the [[Breakwater Fingers]] before reaching Goslow.

| Conditions | Typical Elapsed Time |
| --- | ---: |
| Exceptional wind, tide, and immediate departure | **2–3 days underway** |
| Ordinary passage including a weather or pilot wait | **4–7 days** |
| Poor season or repeated adverse weather | **7+ days** |

Sea travel can beat the road, but it is not a guaranteed shortcut. Passage depends on weather, tides, a suitable vessel, cargo priority, pilots, and a captain willing to sail. Small open boats normally remain close to shore and do not treat the crossing as routine.

## Goslow to Port Quartz

There is **no normal safe overland road between [[Goslow]] and [[Port Quartz]]**. The principal civilized route is by ship: leave Goslow through the western gap in the Breakwater Fingers, clear the guarded cliff waters beneath Pyrris and [[Queen's Gate]], then enter the Coldwater and work upstream to Port Quartz.

**Safe water distance:** approximately **135–150 miles**.

| Vessel or Passage | Goslow to Port Quartz, Upstream | Port Quartz to Goslow, Downstream |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Fast packet in favorable conditions | **4–6 days** | **2–3 days** |
| Ordinary merchant vessel | **7–12 days** | **3–5 days** |
| Heavy barge or awkward cargo | **10–15+ days** | **4–7 days** |
| [[The Storm Runner]] with its regular crew and contracted river hands | **5–8 days** | **2–4 days** |

The Storm Runner normally sails with **24 officers and crew**. That is enough to handle a fast, relatively light seagoing transport, but not enough to drag her bodily against the Coldwater for days. On the upstream run she hires licensed pilots, bank-hauling gangs, and capstan crews along the prepared reaches maintained by [[Port Quartz]]. Her own boats can still kedge or tow through short difficult approaches, but sustained cordelling is shore labor. A practical round trip is **10–16 days** including loading, contracted hauling, and ordinary weather; a contrary wind, unusually strong current, or unavailable river crew can extend it.

### Lilya, the Lake, and the Fall Casks

[[Lilya]] stands at hex **106.81**. Broad-beamed lake craft carry casks and other durable freight roughly **85 miles** west to [[Great Fall]] at hex **89.84.** A small fallhead station at **89.83** sorts loads and transfers them to the protected release works.

| Lake Passage | Typical Time |
| --- | ---: |
| Favorable light craft | **2–3 days** |
| Loaded cask convoy | **3–5 days** |
| Stormbound or ice-threatened passage | **Variable; crews shelter rather than press on** |

Boats never approach the brink. Booms, capstans, guide cables, and shore crews move sealed casks into controlled release lanes. Below the falls, crews at the paired [[Cataract Yard|Cataract Yards]] recover casks on both banks. [[South Cataract Yard]] and [[Westwater Ferry]] occupy **81.95**; [[North Cataract Yard]] occupies **82.94**. Most workers ferry south at sunset because the northern works lack both Port Quartz's comforts and its dependable protection.

## Southern Crossings and Routes

Southern Saros is not a web of interchangeable roads. Its rivers and long interior waters divide the continent into broad regions, and the maintained route system depends on two controlled chokepoints.

- The **Albyrt–Isoltide ferry** is the only known safe crossing of the upper southern divide. There is **no road around the lake**. A traveler avoiding the ferry must provide a suitable boat and cross the immense river that runs from the northern end of [[Vy'tungavor]] to [[The Sparkling Sea]].
- Farther south, [[Soryn's Crossing]] occupies both sides of the river flowing from [[Lake Runa]]. The town controls a great natural land bridge and the maintained passage toward the southern coast.
- Away from those crossings, an apparent straight line on Perla's map may conceal a major water barrier, trackless forest, mountain approach, or pirate-controlled coast. Such travel is an expedition, not an alternate road.

These routes leave the Legion strongest at settlements, ferries, bridges, and road stations. Between them, southern villages and farms rely heavily on local defense, boat owners, and whatever aid the [[The Black Circle|Black Circle]] can provide.

# Regional Economies

## Kylor, Skaggerty, and Goslow

The three western settlements share a timber economy without duplicating one another.

- **[[Kylor]]** selects and seasons old-growth marine timber: keels, masts, spars, ribs, long beams, pitch, tar, resin, and barrel staves. Its boatyards build rugged fishing boats, pilot cutters, and work craft. Kylori weather-readers, coastal pilots, wreck crews, and rescue boatmen are as valuable as the timber itself.
- **[[Skaggerty]]** is the farm-belt market: grain, fodder, livestock, mills, granaries, wagon teams, coppiced firewood, charcoal, fence posts, and ordinary building timber. It supplies Kylor's inland camps and carries Kylori marine timber toward Goslow.
- **[[Goslow]]** finances, finishes, rigs, and launches prestigious vessels. [[Jarvis Aucien]] and other shipwrights buy Kylori keels and spars, then add specialized labor, sailcloth, rope, fittings, credit, and crews.

Goslow's eastward land before the Coldwater is a mix of grain field, pasture, and open steppe. Together with Skaggerty's belt, coastal imports, and the fisheries of Bishop's Bay, it keeps the city fed.

## Bishop's Bay and the Breakwater Fingers

The great rock formations called the [[Breakwater Fingers]] shelter Goslow's inner harbor while restricting entry. The only generally safe approach from Bishop's Bay enters from the west. Pilots, signal stations, tide windows, towboats, lighters, and salvage crews turn that difficult entrance into part of Goslow's economy.

Within the Fingers, open boats of roughly **18–24 feet** can tend sheltered fisheries. Career crews working the main bay generally favor decked craft around **30–45 feet** with crews of three to six, enough hull and hands to survive rough water without requiring every fisherman to own a great ship.

## Lilya and Greyweald Fuel

Lilya is built in terraces against the Silvervein mountainside: lower docks, ore yards, warehouses, and cooperages; middle homes, retaining walls, and avalanche sheds; upper mines, cranes, furnaces, and foundries.

A hard seasonal outpost near the [[The Greyweald|Greyweald]] cuts dense “bentwood” for charcoal. Lilyan smelters prize it because it burns fiercely and evenly, allowing high furnace temperatures with less bulk than ordinary charcoal. Only the hardiest cutters and guards remain there. Fuel and ore cross the Lake of Lament in the same broad-beamed working fleet.

Some burners report strange-colored ash, shared dreams, or metal that seems to hold heat too long. Lilya's guilds blame mineral impurities, exhaustion, and forest superstition; no accepted cause is known.

# Griffin Travel

[[Griffin Riders]] are rare individuals, not a routine transport service or a familiar patrol over every city. A rider can ignore river barriers and may cover **70–100 miles in a day** under useful conditions, but weather, landing sites, the griffin's enormous food needs, rest, and the rider's bond all matter. Emergency flights belong to military crises, royal messages, or exceptional adventures; commercial routes and ordinary itineraries should not list “griffin” beside wagon and ship.

# Working Canon Summary

> Saros uses a working scale of approximately **5 miles per small hex** and **30 miles per large hex**. The mapped land is approximately **500–600 miles across** and **600–700 miles north-to-south**, with a deliberate cartographic error allowance.
>
> Kylor reaches Goslow by road only through Skaggerty. That full road journey is approximately **175–185 miles**, not a two-day walk.
>
> The ordinary Goslow–Port Quartz connection is a rough **135–150-mile** water route. Upstream ships sail, tow, cordelle, warp, and kedge rather than use engines.
>
> Named settlements and strongholds contain about **73,600 people**. The working total population is approximately **300,000**.
>
> Legion control is strongest in towns and along maintained road corridors. Beyond them, villages depend chiefly on their own people and, sometimes, the Black Circle.
>
> Saros should feel like fortified islands of civilization connected by roads, rivers, ports, ferries, and sea lanes through an older and more dangerous world.
